    A multi-outcome study of the presence — or absence — of shared statistical structure between the geometry of the sky and high-salience events on Earth.

    §0 — Statement of falsifiability¶

    This protocol exists to make a negative result possible. Every choice that follows is fixed before looking at the data and will not be revised afterwards.

    Binding commitment. It is assumed that the instruments used — high-precision numerical ephemerides and high-dimensional learned representations — are able to read the hypothesised information.

    It follows that a null result counts as evidence against the hypothesis. It will not be explained away as an insufficiency of the instrument, nor as an inadequacy of the quantitative method to capture the qualitative datum. If the primary hypotheses do not meet the criteria of §4, the study reports a null and closes.

    0.1 Position on mechanism¶

    The study does not assert or assume a causal mechanism. Acausal correspondence, a shared generative law and a common cause produce the same measurable prediction: mutual information greater than zero between the configuration of the sky and the event. The protocol measures that quantity and leaves the metaphysical question open. The only exception is H4, which tests a specific mechanism by making it distinguishable from the others.

    §1 — Primary hypotheses¶

    Five hypotheses, not one more. Everything that does not appear here is exploratory, must be labelled as such in the final report, and needs independent replication in the hold-out before it can be mentioned as a result.

    H1 — Aspects channel¶

    The distribution of the pairwise angular separations between the selected bodies, at the moments of high-salience events, differs from the time-shift null distribution.

    • Primary statistic. Directional harmonic, independent of the orb: C_n = mean of cos(n · Δ), with Δ the signed longitude difference. C_n > 0 means clustering at the phases predicted by harmonic n (n=2 conjunction/opposition, n=3 trines, n=4 squares, n=6 sextiles). One degree of freedom per harmonic: the modulus of the resultant would cost two degrees and would measure clustering at any phase, whereas astrology predicts a precise one. With small effects, directional power is everything.
    • Estimator. Regression of the anomaly of the daily counts on the harmonic anomaly of the sky, unfiltered, with a global time-shift null of radius ±40 years (§4.1, §4.2).
    • Mandatory reporting per pair class (Moon, Mercury, slow): the aggregate statistic is dominated by the Moon and on its own would be misleading.
    • Interpretable secondary. Count of pairs within a 3° orb of the major aspects.
    • Falsification. Observed C_n within the central 95% of the surrogate distribution and a confidence interval that excludes the MEI of §4.3.

    H2 — Exactness, dose–response¶

    The excess detected in H1 grows monotonically as the distance from the exact aspect shrinks.

    • Statistic. Trend test over the declared orb bands: 0–1°, 1–2°, 2–3°, 3–5°, 5–8°.
    • Why it matters. It is the strongest test in the protocol: a spurious correlation has no reason at all to be monotone in exactness. A positive H1 with a flat H2 is almost certainly an artefact.
    • Falsification. Absence of a monotone trend, or a trend in the opposite direction.

    H3 — Angular channel¶

    The continuous angular position of the fast bodies relative to the four angles, computed at the exact coordinates and time of the event, differs from the null distribution.

    • Form. Continuous diurnal phase built on the semi-arc — Gauquelin's sector construction: 0° rise, 90° upper culmination, 180° set, 270° lower culmination. No house system in the primary test: the angular distance from the angles is identical in all systems.
    • Constraint. Needs timestamps with a precision ≤ 15 minutes. Applicable only to the datasets of §2.3 and §2.4.
    • Falsification. Uniformity with respect to the null not rejected, effect below the MEI.

    H4 — Mechanism discriminant (conditional)¶

    The effect sizes of H1–H3 are larger for human-mediated events than for geophysical events, matched for number, geographic distribution and hourly distribution.

    • Interpretation. It is the test of the psychic-mediation model: an earthquake has no inner life. If the information passes through human subjectivity, the contrast must exist.
    • Condition. Interpretable only if H1 or H3 turn out positive. With H1 and H3 null, H4 is not computed.
    • Falsification. Effects statistically indistinguishable between the two classes, or larger for the geophysical class. In that case the mediation model falls, even if H1 survives.

    H5 — Semantic channel¶

    The numeric features of the sky predict the position of the event in the embedding space above the permutation null.

    • Form. Forced-choice retrieval: given the chart, rank K = 20 candidate event descriptions. Metric: out-of-sample MRR against the known chance baseline (1/K).
    • Why this way. Out-of-sample prediction above chance is mutual information, and it cannot be attacked with "you fitted the model": the test set has never been seen.
    • Falsification. Out-of-sample MRR within the central 95% of the permutation null.

    §2 — Data¶

    2.1 Inclusion criterion — the critical point¶

    Gauquelin's historical failure was not sample size: it was the eminence criterion, that is, who got into the sample. That bias is fully present here in the form of "which events are important".

    Binding rule. The inclusion criterion is defined by third-party measures, blind to astrology, computable by a script. No human judgement and no model judgement enters the selection of the sample. The thresholds are fixed here and are not touched again.

    2.2 Human-mediated events — primary¶

    Item Specification
    Source GDELT 2.0 Event Database
    Window 19 Feb 2015 → registration date. 1.0 is never merged with 2.0: different coding, confounding
    Salience NumSources ≥ 10, NumMentions ≥ 25, abs(GoldsteinScale) ≥ 5.0
    Geocoding ActionGeo_Type ≥ 2 (ADM1 or better) with lat/lon present
    Resolution Daily. Aspects channel only — DATEADDED is the publication time and is never used as the time of the event
    Robustness ACLED as an independent replication on conflicts and unrest

    [Translator's note: the salience thresholds were changed to NumSources ≥ 4, NumMentions ≥ 25 by amendment #16 before the v0.4 freeze.]

    2.3 Events with an exact timestamp — angular channel¶

    Market series anchored to a physical exchange: millisecond timestamps, a geographically fixed location, continuous magnitude. The events are price jumps or realised volatility beyond a pre-declared quantile. It is the only dataset that feeds both channels, and it is by construction the collective human psyche.

    [Translator's note: amendment #18 restricted the markets arm to daily closes, so it does not feed H3; amendment #20 limits H3 to USGS.]

    2.4 Geophysical control — negative¶

    USGS Earthquake Catalog, M ≥ 5.5, from 1990. Exact UTC time, exact coordinates, continuous magnitude, zero editorial bias. It serves as the comparison class for H4, not as the main test.

    Note from Phase 0. Since power depends on the number of days and not of events, the geophysical arm (36 years) has more power than the human arm (11.5 years). It is a counterintuitive fact and must be declared: the negative control is the more sensitive of the two.

    2.5 Validation structure¶

    The split is random, stratified by year, region and event type, not temporal: a hold-out by date would have a different planetary era and would make every failed replication ambiguous.

    Set Share Function Seal
    Exploration 60% Development, iteration, exploratory analyses Open
    Validation 20% First confirmation on the primary hypotheses only Opened once only
    Prospective 20% + future Events after the registration Minimum 6 months of accumulation

    The prospective set is the strongest guarantee available: it runs on events that did not exist when the protocol was written. No form of leakage is possible. Split seed fixed and published.

    [Translator's note: amendment #22 set the split unit to the calendar month, stratified by year.]

    §3 — Astronomical variables¶

    3.1 Engine and conventions¶

    • Swiss Ephemeris via pyswisseph, Moshier ephemerides: deterministic, with no external files, so reproducible by anyone without downloading anything.
    • astro-engine is not used. The code of this study is a minimal numeric layer written from scratch: no interpretive code path, no planet or aspect name that could leak into a feature, and dependencies reduced to pyswisseph and numpy. It is a deliberate choice of anti-contamination hygiene (§3.4). A cross-check remains to be done: compare longitudes, separations and cusps with astro-engine on a sample of dates, and report the maximum deviation. Until it is done, the two engines are not declared equivalent.
    • All times in UTC, with Delta-T correction.
    • Apparent geocentric positions, with the exception of the Moon, computed topocentrically: the lunar parallax reaches ~1° (measured: 0.62° in a test case), not negligible against a 3° orb.

    [Translator's note: SPEC V (V2) later moved the study onto astro-engine; the cross-check is the § 6.1 gate of SPEC V, passed on 2026-09-05.]

    3.2 Bodies and admitted pairs¶

    Primary set: Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn. 14 admitted pairs.

    Decorrelation rule. Every pair must contain at least one fast body (Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars). It is the fast body that decorrelates the angle within the horizon of the time shift. Pairs of two slow bodies — Jupiter–Saturn above all — are excluded: their angle does not vary enough within the window to be separated from the secular trend.

    The Sun is excluded from the primary test. Its longitude is an almost deterministic function of the day of the year: a surrogate that preserves the season also preserves the Sun, so the pairs that contain it do not decorrelate and their effect is inseparable from seasonality. They enter only in Tier 2 and are declared secondary.

    3.3 Aspects and orb¶

    Major aspects: 0°, 60°, 90°, 120°, 180°. Primary orb 3°, single and declared. Sensitivity curve at 1°, 2°, 3°, 5°, 8° reported as secondary — declared now, so it cannot be selected afterwards. The primary statistic of H1 is in any case independent of the orb.

    3.4 Encoding — anti-contamination constraint¶

    The language model has read astrology. If the sky side touches natural language, the associations learned from the manuals generate correlation by themselves and produce a perfect false positive.

    • ✕ No name of a planet, aspect, house or sign ever enters a feature or a prompt.
    • ✕ The sky side is numeric only: separations in degrees, positions as sine and cosine.
    • ✕ The event descriptions are cleaned of proper names, dates and references that identify a known event — otherwise the embedding encodes when, and therefore the sky.
    • ✕ H5 is replicated with at least two distinct model families: two groups that use the same model are not independent replications.

    3.5 Explicitly excluded¶

    The zodiac signs. Aspects are invariant under the ayanamsa — the difference of two longitudes does not depend on the offset — so the tropical/sidereal question does not arise and a whole degree of freedom disappears together with a whole class of objection.

    House systems in the primary test. Equal houses from the Ascendant and Placidus appear only as declared robustness checks, and they count in the multiplicity correction. Events beyond 60° of latitude are analysed separately for Placidus, which degenerates at high latitudes. Circumpolar bodies are excluded and the count of exclusions is reported.

    Uranus, Neptune, Pluto paired with each other; asteroids; fictitious points; fixed stars; Arabic parts.

    §4 — Analysis¶

    4.1 Time-shift null model¶

    The astronomical variables are massively confounded with calendar and geography. A uniform null would guarantee spurious significance.

    For every event 1000 surrogates are generated with: same coordinates, same clock time, a shift of whole years with ±20 days of jitter, up to ±40 years. The radius must far exceed the data window (11.5 years): at ±6 years the surrogates overlap and the null does not sample the low frequencies, making the test anticonservative (false positives 0.043 against 0.010 nominal).

    Holds fixed Lets vary
    Local solar time, and therefore the confounder with human activity Position of the Moon
    Geography, and therefore the sidereal-time bias from geographic concentration Aspects between fast bodies
    Season (within the jitter) Lunar phase
    Era of recording, volume of coverage Angles with Jupiter and Saturn

    The shift is GLOBAL, not per event. All events shift by the same number of days. An independent shift per event would destroy the autocorrelation of the sky — Mars moves ~0.5°/day, nearby days have almost identical skies — and would produce a null that is too narrow, hence false positives. With the global shift the surrogate preserves exactly the structure of the celestial process. It is the choice that makes the calibration hold.

    Tier 2, robustness. Free shifts of ±[7, 45] days: they also decorrelate the Sun but introduce seasonal drift. The only regime in which the pairs with the Sun are testable; results declared secondary.

    Declared limitation. Any effect with an annual or diurnal signature is partly absorbed by the null. The protocol cannot distinguish it and does not claim it.

    4.2 Primary estimator¶

    Regression of the anomaly of the daily counts on the harmonic anomaly of the sky, unfiltered (the high-pass filter stays as a declared robustness check at 90, 180, 365 days):

    beta_hat  = sum(y~ · c~) / (mean(y) · sum(c~²))
delta_hat = beta_hat · Var(c)

    The generative model is lambda_d ∝ exp(beta · c_d), so for small beta the anomaly of the counts is y~ ≈ mean · beta · c~ + noise. The filter is linear and applies to both sides, so the regression estimates the same beta without loss of signal, while removing the slow drift that made up almost all of the noise.

    Declared robustness windows: 45, 180, 365 days.

    Why the filter is not primary. Once the surrogate radius is corrected, it is no longer needed to defend the calibration, and it is not neutral about what the test sees: a 90-day high-pass keeps 94% of the variance of the pairs with the Moon but 3% of Mars–Jupiter. Keeping it in the primary would silently reweight the test towards the Moon alone.

    4.3 Minimum effect of interest¶

    Anchored to the only effect of known magnitude ever claimed in this field, Gauquelin's Mars effect: 22% observed against 16.7% expected in the key sectors. The derivation (scripts/derive_mei.py) takes as input only those published percentages and no information about our design.
    Displaced fraction eps 0.0640
    Gauquelin magnitude effect C = 0.0611
    MEI = half of Gauquelin C = 0.0306

    4.4 Multiplicity¶

    Five primary hypotheses, Holm–Bonferroni correction at familywise α 0.05: the strictest threshold is α = 0.01. Every declared robustness check (filter windows, house systems, orb bands, second embedding model) enters the count. Everything else is exploratory, controlled by FDR, and is not a result until it replicates in the hold-out.

    4.5 Anti-p-hacking commitments¶

    After seeing any result, there will be no:

    • ✕ change of the primary orb or the primary filter window
    • ✕ addition of house systems
    • ✕ addition or removal of celestial bodies
    • ✕ post-hoc subgroups
    • ✕ exclusion of "anomalous" events by criteria not declared here
    • ✕ change of the salience thresholds
    • ✕ change of the primary metrics

    Every deviation from the protocol is recorded with date and time in docs/AMENDMENTS.md and reported in the final report, positive or negative.

    §5 — Outcomes and their interpretation¶

    The design is built so that no outcome is wasted. The three rows are declared here, before looking at the data, to make after-the-fact reinterpretation impossible.

    Outcome Reading
    Signal on human events, not on geophysical ones Supports the mediation model: the information passes through human subjectivity before manifesting. It is the author's hypothesis, and it is the only configuration that supports it.
    Signal on both to a similar degree Supports a general acausal correspondence and contradicts the mediation model. An interesting result, but against the starting hypothesis.
    No signal A clean, publishable null. With a pre-registered protocol, a null at this scale and with these controls is a contribution to the literature, not a failure.

    §6 — Phase 0: results¶

    Phase 0 did not touch real event data. It used only the sky (deterministic and public) and synthetic daily counts.

    [Translator's note: the tables in §6.1–§6.4 come from the v0.2 Phase 0, when the 90-day filter was primary and the surrogate radius was ±6 years. Amendments #11–#14 (v0.3) changed both; the v0.3 numbers are in results/phase0-ts-compare.md and in amendment #14.]

    6.1 Calibration of the null¶

    With realistic counts — secular drift ±30%, seasonality ±15%, weekly cycle ±20%, persistent AR(1) regimes with φ = 0.97 — and 400 pseudo-real windows:

    Filter False positives at α = 0.05 at α = 0.01 KS uniformity Outcome
    none 0.080 0.040 0.160 disqualified
    365 d 0.065 0.028 0.747 admitted
    180 d 0.045 0.010 0.883 admitted
    90 d (primary) 0.052 0.015 0.851 admitted
    45 d 0.035 0.007 0.720 admitted

    6.2 Recovery of the injected effect¶

    Injected Recovered Std error
    0.000 −0.0004 0.0022
    0.010 0.0101 0.0025
    0.030 0.0326 0.0030
    0.060 0.0722 0.0038

    Unbiased at the scale of the MEI; overestimate of ~20% for large effects, because of the non-linearity of the exponential tilt. Declared: confidence intervals for large effects are to be read as conservative upwards.

    6.3 Minimum detectable effect (80% power, α = 0.01, 90 d filter)¶

    Aggregate over the 14 pairs, per harmonic:

    Harmonic MDE Relative to Gauquelin
    n = 2 0.0088 14%
    n = 3 0.0093 15%
    n = 4 0.0113 19%
    n = 6 0.0083 14%

    But the aggregate is dominated by the Moon. Per pair class (harmonic 2):

    Class Pairs MDE % of Gauquelin Margin over the MEI
    Moon 5 0.0094 15% 3.3×
    Mercury 4 0.0249 41% 1.2×
    slow 5 0.0276 45% 1.1×

    The result must always be reported per class, all three, never selectively.

    6.4 Verdict¶

    GO. MDE ≈ 0.009 against MEI = 0.0306. The design sees effects down to about 14% of Gauquelin's magnitude, with a margin of more than three times over the threshold of interest.

    Limitation to declare at the opening of the final report. Effects smaller than 14% of the Mars effect stay out of reach: on those the study declares that it cannot decide, and a null does not exclude them.

    6.5 What Phase 0 changed in the design¶

    1. The number of events counts for almost nothing. The aspects are geocentric and do not depend on the location; with data at daily resolution all the events of the same day share the same chart, and nearby days have almost identical skies (autocorrelation 30–70 days). The effective sample size is the number of days: 4212, not 100,000 events.
    2. The naive estimator was replaced. The weighted mean of cos(n·Δ) has background noise dominated by the slow drift of the sky. The regression of the filtered anomalies cuts it by a factor of 3 without losing signal.
    3. The unfiltered variant was disqualified. With white noise it looked the best of all; with realistic counts it quadruples the false positives. It is exactly the trap that a non-pre-registered study would never see.

    §7 — Reproducibility¶

    • Extraction queries published in full, executable by third parties.
    • Analysis code in a public repository, under version control.
    • All random seeds fixed and declared (config.SEED = 20260901).
    • Moshier ephemerides: deterministic, no files to download.
    • Construction of the diurnal phase validated against Swiss Ephemeris's independent calculation of rise, culmination and set, to within 3 minutes.
    • Protocol deposited on OSF with a timestamp before Phase 1.

    This is where the study really goes beyond Gauquelin: not in sample size, but in the fact that anyone can regenerate the exact sample starting from public sources.

    §8 — Declared limitations¶

    • The slow planets are not testable with this design. The study says nothing about Uranus, Neptune and Pluto.
    • The Sun is not testable in the primary, because of structural confounding with seasonality.
    • Effects below 14% of Gauquelin's magnitude are out of reach.
    • The daily resolution of GDELT and ACLED confines the angular channel to the subset of data with exact timestamps.
    • Power depends on the structure of the real counts. Phase 0 uses a plausible model; if the real counts had more low-frequency power, power would drop. Declared remedy: Phase 2a, recalibration with the real marginal structure of the counts before any sky–event pairing. Looking at the autocorrelation of Y without looking at Y against the sky is not peeking.
    • Embeddings are a bridge, not an identity. They encode semantic proximity as learned by a model, which coincides neither with the qualitative information of the hypothesis nor with Shannon information, which deliberately excludes semantics. It is an operationalisation choice.
    • Shared systematic bias of the models: the model removes the idiosyncrasy of the single researcher but introduces priors common to everyone who uses the same model.
    • Narrow coverage of the tradition: a null on this protocol is a null on these hypotheses, not on astrology in general — and the report will say so explicitly, without using it as a loophole.
